About Me

Hello, I’m Ryan — a recent mechanical engineering graduate who loves turning ideas into tangible solutions. Whether I’m sketching a mechanism on paper or prototyping parts in the lab, I'm excited by how things work and how they can be improved. Over the past few years, I’ve designed automation equipment for a manufacturing line, helped upgrade HVAC systems in iconic Boston buildings, and even spent the spring of my junior year in a microfluidics lab focused on acoustofluidics, where sound waves are used to manipulate fluids and particles at the microscale. Those experiences taught me that engineering is as much about listening and teamwork as it is about calculations and CAD. Collaboration makes the work far more rewarding.

Leadership & Service

Beyond technical work, I care deeply about community and academic success. During my year as scholarship chair on the executive board of Theta Chi’s Eta Chapter, I organized study sessions and professional‑development workshops to support my brothers. Those late‑night review sessions and one‑on‑one check‑ins weren’t always glamorous, but they paid off — our chapter’s GPA jumped significantly, and we climbed from the lower ranks to near the top among campus fraternities. Most importantly, I discovered how much I enjoy helping others learn and thrive.

Entrepreneurship & Professional Development

I’ve learned that great ideas only make an impact if people understand and support them. To stretch beyond the workshop, I recently completed Theta Chi’s Resolute Man Experience, an intensive weekend program designed for a small cohort of eight to ten of my fraternity brothers from various majors. Led by two seasoned mentors—Devin Cooley, co‑founder of the renewable‑cooling company NVIRIQ, and Jim Valenti, a veteran business trainer and retired U.S. Army officer—we dove into strategic planning, value‑driven sales, negotiation, and leadership exercises. Spending hours debating product pitches which broadened my perspective on how to communicate ideas across disciplines and left me energized about blending technical work with business strategy.

What’s Next
